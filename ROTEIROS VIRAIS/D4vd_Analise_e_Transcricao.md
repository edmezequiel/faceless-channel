\*\*\[00:00:18\]\*\* However, in September of 2025, detectives discovered the body of a 14-year-old girl that had been missing for months, Celeste Rivas Hernandez, in the trunk of his Tesla—decomposed, dismembered, and abandoned.  

\*\*\[00:00:25\]\*\* And for months, people debated on whether they believed that this innocent-looking, clean-shaven young man with a melodic voice could do the things he'd been accused of.    
\*\*\[00:00:38\]\*\* But now that David has been arrested for murder and the prosecution have documented their evidence, the likelihood that this was some sort of setup has been significantly reduced.    
\*\*\[00:00:45\]\*\* It became evidenced that David had a physical relationship with Celeste while she was still a child, and when they broke up and she allegedly threatened to expose the relationship to ruin his career, the prosecution believe he took matters into his own hands to protect what he'd built.

\*\*\[00:01:32\]\*\* It's easy to just label him as pure evil and move on. But as a student of psychology, I find it crucial that we understand what happened to him in childhood, what were the warning signs, and figure out the "why" that surely exists in this story.

\*\*\[00:02:13\]\*\* Let's start from the beginning.    
\> \*Interviewer:\* "So you're a New Yorker?"    
\> \*David:\* "New Yorker, Queens, New York."

\*\*\[00:02:30\]\*\* David Anthony Burke was born in Queens, New York, to Colleen and Dwood Burke, two Christian parents that seemingly would give him a tough and controlled upbringing.    
\> \*David:\* "Everything we played in the house music-wise was just Bach, Beethoven, Mozart, gospel music, like classical stuff, instrumentals only. Like we didn't listen to any other music. There was no like Lady Gaga and all the popular artists everybody was listening to. We never could listen to that cuz my parents just wouldn't let us. It was just like straight classical stuff... cuz my parents wanted me to be really smart... always wanted me to be very intellectual, very put together..."  

\*\*\[00:03:13\]\*\* Trying to control what your kids listen to and the content they consume isn't necessarily harmful in and of itself. But if a kid is going to school and has no level of cultural relatability, it can cause feelings of being excluded, which in turn affects his socialization.    
\*\*\[00:03:31\]\*\* Instead of David accepting this, he tried to find a way around it.    
\> \*Interviewer:\* "Did your parents put sort of a cap on what you could and couldn't listen to in the house?"    
\> \*David:\* "I used to sneak around with the iPod touch and download things off iTunes and stuff like that."  

\*\*\[00:03:50\]\*\* Sneaking around his parents like this is consistent with Reactance Theory proposed by Jack Brehm in 1966\. When perceived behavioral freedom is restricted, individuals are motivated to reclaim it. In these situations, the forbidden fruit becomes more desirable and the individual tries to acquire it by any means, even if it includes secrecy.    
\*\*\[00:04:12\]\*\* Now, it's not the behavior itself that's necessarily significant, but the pattern it establishes: that authority is to be managed through concealment rather than confronted directly. This pattern would then reoccur later through his adult life.  

\*\*\[00:04:20\]\*\* It didn't help David that his private school life in New York also consisted of bullying and teachers picking on him.    
\> \*David:\* "I hated New York at that point. Like I didn't like my school. I was at a private school and they treated me so horribly, dude... All the teachers had it out for me... they would just pick on me and bully me for no reason..."  

\*\*\[00:04:54\]\*\* And he got his wish as they moved to Houston, Texas, and he found it slightly better at his new school. But he was a kid that didn't really seem to find anything that stuck. His parents tried to get him into music, but it wasn't stimulating enough for him at that time.    
\> \*David:\* "She put me in piano lessons when I was seven and then I quit that after a week... put me in a church choir when I was eight, quit that. Middle school band in seventh grade, I played the flute and I quit that."  

\*\*\[00:05:29\]\*\* But he never developed into a conventionally social child. He describes himself as a "people watcher" rather than a member of a friend group, sitting apart in cafeteria settings and constructing fictional narratives around classmates that they were unaware of.    
\> \*David:\* "At school before I was homeschooled... lunch was like my brain's playground. I'm a people watcher. I would just inspect them and write about them... Every time I would write a short story, I'd have these different classmates in these stories and they would never know..."  

\*\*\[00:06:11\]\*\* This preference to observe people and create these stories was an early marker that he had a natural tendency to see people as subjects for analysis rather than reciprocal social partners.    
\*\*\[00:06:29\]\*\* He says he began writing poetry in the fifth grade and pursued anime, gaming, and creative fiction from a young age.    
\> \*David:\* "Ever since I was young, I really just liked anime, gaming, and poetry... Dragon Ball, Tokyo Ghoul... see these fantasy characters with powers... that inspired me to start writing my own stories about them."  

\*\*\[00:06:52\]\*\* But just in case his parents couldn't give him any more reason to resent them, they took him out of public school—the only place he was getting any sort of socialization, the place where he was letting his imagination run wild—and they instead placed him on an isolated homeschool curriculum.    
\> \*David:\* "Transitioning from public school to homeschool was tragic... I was asking to go back to school almost every day... It was strictly on computer. That's what I did."  

\*\*\[00:07:49\]\*\* From a developmental psychology standpoint, this is the most significant single environmental factor in his profile. Erikson's eight-stage model identifies adolescence (approximately 12 to 18 years old) as the critical period for identity versus role confusion. Removing a child from that process during its peak window doesn't simply slow development—it redirects it.  

\*\*\[00:08:27\]\*\* None of his peers stayed in contact with him following the transition, and he was all alone:    
\> \*David:\* "I thought I had a lot of friends in middle school, and then after being homeschooled, nobody's hitting me up... It made me question what is a real friend and do I have any of those... Homeschooling really takes a toll on a kid, like isolation."  

\*\*\[00:09:25\]\*\* David would start to feel a growing disconnect between himself and his agemates. He missed out on high school relationships and experiences, watching everyone else through social media.    
\*\*\[00:10:24\]\*\* When adolescents are denied normal peer socialization, the need for connection finds alternative channels. In David's case, that channel was the internet: \*Fortnite\*, YouTube, Discord, TikTok, and eventually music.  

\*\*\[00:10:41\]\*\* During his homeschool years, David describes \*Fortnite\* as his primary social environment, letting strangers vent their personal problems to him:    
\> \*David:\* "I would go on Discord and Fortnite squad fills and just let people talk to me... I'm like 14, 15 at this time... I'm taking this information and thinking, 'Wow, so this is what humans are like. These are humans.'"  

\*\*\[00:11:45\]\*\* This is a clinically unusual developmental pathway. Normally an adolescent's model of human behavior is built through direct, embodied, reciprocal experience. But David built his model of human psychology through a parasocial and semi-anonymous listening role. He understands humans like a researcher understands subjects, not as a participant understands peers.  

\*\*\[00:12:38\]\*\* In his own Discord server, David disclosed a sustained pattern of consuming gore content beginning at approximately 14 years old following accidental exposure to a cartel execution video.    
\*\*\[00:13:08\]\*\* He described a progression from initial shock to habituation, claiming:    
\> \*"I watch gore to disconnect the part of my brain that feels empathy to the wrong things."\*  

\*\*\[00:13:20\]\*\* Repeated exposure to graphic stimuli triggers habituation and desensitization in the amygdala. What distinguishes David's disclosure is that he used it as an emotional engineering exercise—a deliberate attempt to modify his own empathic response.  

\*\*\[00:14:24\]\*\* During that same isolated period, David developed an elaborate alter ego he called \*Itami\* (Japanese for "pain"):    
\> \*David:\* "Itami actually started years before music, when I was writing my first comic book. It was this detective that would solve his own crimes. He was the good guy by day, and then at night he'd transform into this alter ego and commit crimes he'd solve the morning of..."  

\*\*\[00:15:34\]\*\* While escapist fantasy uses fictional scenarios to temporarily relieve stress, \*fantasy elaboration\* constructs immersive parallel frameworks for identity. David explicitly inserted himself into this narrative as an agent of chaos.  

\*\*\[00:16:31\]\*\* David transitioned into music accidentally in late 2021 after copyright strikes on his gaming content prompted his mother to suggest he create original music:    
\> \*David:\* "The only reason I started making music is because my videos got copyrighted... My mom was like, 'Make your own song.' So I kept doing it..."  

\*\*\[00:17:33\]\*\* Music gave his internal world a public, socially legitimate form. Dark themes wrapped in music register as artistry rather than warning signs.    
\*\*\[00:18:31\]\*\* His early song \*Romantic Homicide\* (2022) contained the central lyric: \*"I killed you in the back of my mind."\*    
\> \*David:\* "You think that I'm going to say 'you died', but I say 'I killed you'... It was something that I decided to do."    
\> \*David:\* "I played it for my mother in the car and she was like, 'David, this song is too violent. You talking about killing people in your music?' ... I was like, 'Wait, I might be onto something.'"  

\*\*\[00:20:04\]\*\* The song became his commercial breakthrough, earning him millions of streams and a record deal with Interscope. From a behavioral reinforcement standpoint, externalizing dark fantasy produced the outcome most desired: status, belonging, and recognition.  

\*\*\[00:21:14\]\*\* He constructed an entire identity architecture around himself with four defined personas:    
1\. D4vd (the artist)    
2\. Itami (the antagonist)    
3\. His gaming persona    
4\. His social media persona  

The authentic self was not among them, reflecting identity diffusion.

\*\*\[00:34:52\]\*\* Clinical Questions Answered:    
1\. \*\*Nature vs. Nurture:\*\* Genetic disposition combined with 5 years of total social isolation created an environment where dark impulses developed without social correction.    
2\. \*\*Why was David different from other isolated teens?\*\* The extreme combination of total isolation \+ deliberate gore desensitization \+ lack of peer feedback \+ commercial celebration of dark themes.    
3\. \*\*Why leave the body in the Tesla?\*\* Narcissistic overconfidence and belief in his own intelligence to manage risks.    
4\. \*\*How did he function normally afterward?\*\* Reduced guilt response, compartmentalization, and switching to his "artist persona".    
5\. \*\*Would he have killed again?\*\* While not compulsion-driven like a serial killer, the low psychological barrier to eliminating threats to his career made reoffending under similar circumstances likely.
